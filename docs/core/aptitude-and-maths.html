➗ Aptitude & Maths

Round 1 of almost every company. The cheapest round to clear — and the one most students fail.#

🏠 Home • 📚 Core skills • 🧮 DSA

Why you cannot skip this#

Thousands of students with good coding skills get rejected in round 1 every year because they didn't practise percentages and time-and-work.

  • Every service company (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture, Capgemini) puts aptitude first. Fail it and your coding ability is never even seen.
  • Many product companies include aptitude or quant sections in their OAs.
  • It's the highest return per hour of any prep you'll do. 60 hours of practice reliably takes you from failing to comfortably clearing. Compare that to DSA, which takes 600.

Important

These are class 8–10 maths topics. You already learned all of it. You've just forgotten it and you're slow. Aptitude is a speed test, not a difficulty test — 30 questions in 30 minutes means one minute each, including reading.

The three sections#

SectionWeightWhat it covers
Quantitative Aptitude~40%Arithmetic, algebra, geometry, data interpretation
Logical Reasoning~35%Puzzles, series, arrangements, coding-decoding
Verbal Ability~25%Grammar, vocabulary, reading comprehension

📐 Quantitative Aptitude#

High-priority topics (learn these first — ~70% of questions)#

  • Percentages ⭐ — the foundation of profit/loss, interest, and data interpretation
  • Ratio & Proportion ⭐
  • Averages ⭐
  • Time, Speed & Distance ⭐ — including trains, boats and streams
  • Time & Work ⭐ — including pipes and cisterns
  • Profit, Loss & Discount ⭐
  • Simple & Compound Interest ⭐
  • Number System ⭐ — divisibility, HCF/LCM, remainders, unit digits
  • Permutation & Combination ⭐
  • Probability ⭐

Medium priority#

  • Mixtures & Alligation
  • Ages
  • Partnership
  • Mensuration — area, volume, surface area
  • Geometry — triangles, circles, coordinate geometry
  • Progressions — AP, GP
  • Algebra — linear and quadratic equations
  • Logarithms
  • Clocks & Calendars (TCS loves these)
  • Data Interpretation ⭐ — tables, bar charts, pie charts, line graphs
📌 Shortcuts and formulas worth memorising

Percentages → fractions (learn these; they save enormous time)

%Fraction%Fraction
12.5%1/833.33%1/3
16.67%1/637.5%3/8
20%1/550%1/2
25%1/462.5%5/8
6.25%1/1666.67%2/3

Time & Work: if A does a job in a days and B in b days, together they take ab/(a+b) days. Better method: assume total work = LCM(a, b) units.

Speed: km/hr → m/s, multiply by 5/18. m/s → km/hr, multiply by 18/5.

Trains: a train of length L crossing a pole covers L. Crossing a platform of length P covers L + P.

Successive percentage change: net change = a + b + (ab/100).

Compound Interest: A = P(1 + r/100)^n. For 2 years, CI − SI = P(r/100)².

Averages: if the average of n numbers changes by x when one number is replaced, the difference between the two numbers is n × x.

Squares to 30 and cubes to 15 — memorise them. They appear constantly and save 20 seconds each.

🧩 Logical Reasoning#

  • Number & letter series ⭐ — find the next term
  • Coding-decoding ⭐
  • Blood relations ⭐
  • Direction sense
  • Seating arrangement ⭐ — linear and circular (the highest-scoring topic if you practise)
  • Puzzles ⭐ — floor puzzles, scheduling, box arrangements
  • Syllogisms ⭐ — use Venn diagrams, always
  • Statement & conclusion / assumption
  • Data sufficiency
  • Cubes & dice
  • Clocks & calendars
  • Analogies and odd-one-out
  • Venn diagrams
  • Non-verbal — figure series, mirror images, paper folding

Tip

Seating arrangement and puzzles carry the most marks and are the most learnable. They look intimidating but they're pure method: draw a diagram, place the certain information first, then eliminate. Practise 50 of them and they become mechanical.

📖 Verbal Ability#

  • Reading comprehension ⭐ — read the questions before the passage
  • Grammar — subject-verb agreement, tenses, articles, prepositions, modifiers
  • Error spotting ⭐
  • Sentence correction / improvement
  • Para jumbles ⭐ — reorder sentences into a paragraph
  • Sentence completion / fill in the blanks
  • Synonyms & antonyms — build vocabulary steadily
  • Idioms and phrases
  • Critical reasoning

Vocabulary: learn 10 words a day using Magoosh Vocabulary Builder or Word Power Made Easy. Reading English news daily (The Hindu, Indian Express) does most of the work for you.

📅 The 8-week plan (1 hour a day)#

WeekFocusDaily
1Percentages, ratio, averagesLearn concept (20 min) + 30 questions
2Time-speed-distance, time-workLearn (20 min) + 30 questions
3Profit-loss, interest, number systemLearn (20 min) + 30 questions
4P&C, probability, mixtures, agesLearn (20 min) + 30 questions
5Series, coding-decoding, blood relations, directions40 questions
6Seating arrangement, puzzles, syllogisms30 questions (these take longer)
7Verbal — grammar, RC, para jumbles + Data Interpretation40 questions
8Full-length mock tests, timed2 mocks + review every mistake

Week 8 is the most important week. Timed full mocks expose your real weaknesses — usually speed, not knowledge.

⏱️ Speed technique (this is what the test actually measures)#

  1. Never solve a question fully if you can eliminate options. Options are a gift — use them.
  2. Approximate. If options are 240, 380, 420, 590 — you don't need exact arithmetic.
  3. Skip aggressively. If a question isn't yielding in 60 seconds, mark and move on. There is always an easier question later.
  4. Do the easy sections first. Scan the paper, harvest the quick marks, come back.
  5. Practise mental maths daily — tables to 20, squares to 30, cubes to 15, common fractions.
  6. Watch for negative marking. If it's negative-marked, only guess when you've eliminated two options.
  7. Time-box each section. Don't spend 25 of your 30 minutes on quant and rush verbal.

🧮 Maths for specific tracks#

Maths you need for Data / AI / ML

Beyond aptitude, this track needs real mathematics:

Statistics & Probability ⭐ (most tested)

  • Descriptive statistics — mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation
  • Distributions — normal, binomial, Poisson
  • Central Limit Theorem
  • Hypothesis testing, p-values, confidence intervals, t-tests, chi-square
  • Type I and Type II errors
  • Bayes' theorem, conditional probability
  • Correlation vs causation, sampling bias
  • A/B testing design

Linear Algebra

  • Vectors, matrices, matrix multiplication
  • Dot product, transpose, inverse, determinant
  • Eigenvalues and eigenvectors (PCA)

Calculus

  • Derivatives, partial derivatives
  • Chain rule, gradients (backpropagation)
  • Optimisation — maxima, minima, gradient descent

Best free resources: StatQuest for statistics · 3Blue1Brown for linear algebra and calculus intuition · Khan Academy

📖 tracks/data-ai-ml.md

Maths you need for general software engineering

Less than you'd think, but these come up:

  • Discrete maths — sets, relations, functions, logic
  • Combinatorics — permutations and combinations (for DSA problems)
  • Number theory — modular arithmetic, GCD/LCM, primes (competitive programming and some interview problems)
  • Big-O and asymptotic analysis — logarithms, growth rates
  • Boolean algebra — for bit manipulation
  • Basic probability — for hashing, load balancing, randomised algorithms

You do not need calculus for standard software development. Don't let anyone tell you otherwise.

⚠️ Aptitude mistakes#

MistakeFix
Starting 2 weeks before the test8 weeks minimum. It's a speed skill and speed takes reps.
"I'm good at maths, I'll manage"You're good at solving. This tests solving in 60 seconds. Different skill.
Learning concepts without timed practiceAlways practise with a clock from week 2.
Skipping verbalIt's 25% of the paper and the easiest to improve.
Not doing full mocksSection-wise practice hides your pacing problems.
Ignoring previous-year questionsService companies repeat patterns heavily. This is free marks.
Not reviewing wrong answersThe review is where the learning is. Redo every mistake the next day.
Solving everything longhandLearn shortcuts and fraction equivalents. They halve your time.
