## Why DSA decides your placement

DSA is the **only universal filter**. Every product company tests it, and it's the one part of hiring where your college genuinely doesn't matter — an online assessment is a text editor and some test cases.

It's also not about being smart. **There are roughly 30 patterns.** Once you've internalised them, most interview problems become "oh, that's sliding window with a hashmap." Getting there takes about 300 problems. There's no shortcut, and there doesn't need to be — it's just work.

---

## How many DSA questions are enough for placement?

<div align="center">

| Target | Problems | Difficulty mix | Timeline |
|---|:---:|---|---|
| **Service companies** | 150–200 | 70% Easy, 30% Medium | 4–6 months |
| **Mid product / startups** | 300–400 | 40% E, 55% M, 5% Hard | 8–12 months |
| **Strong product** | 450–550 | 25% E, 60% M, 15% H | 12–18 months |
| **FAANG-level** | 600–700 | 20% E, 60% M, 20% H | 18–24 months |

</div>

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **300 problems you can re-solve beats 800 you copied from editorials.** If you can't solve a problem again two weeks later without help, it doesn't count toward your total.

## Which language should you pick?

| Language | Pick it if | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **C++** | You want maximum DSA strength | Best STL, fastest execution, standard in contests |
| **Java** | You're targeting Indian mass hiring | Most common in service + enterprise job postings |
| **Python** | You want the gentlest start, or lean data/ML | Cleanest syntax; occasionally restricted in OAs |

**Pick one and stay with it for 12 months.** The language isn't the skill — the patterns are. Switching resets your muscle memory for no benefit.

## The learning order

Do **not** jump to dynamic programming in month two. DP without solid recursion is memorising solutions you'll forget in a week.

```
FOUNDATION (2-3 months)   Arrays · Strings · Hashing · Sorting · Searching · Recursion
        ↓
LINEAR (1-2 months)       Linked Lists · Stacks · Queues · Two Pointers · Sliding Window
        ↓
TREES (1-2 months)        Binary Trees · BST · Traversals · Heaps
        ↓
GRAPHS (1-2 months)       BFS · DFS · Topological Sort · Union-Find · Shortest Path
        ↓
DP (2-3 months)           1D · 2D · Knapsack · Subsequences
        ↓
ADVANCED (1 month)        Tries · Bit Manipulation · Segment Trees (optional)
```

## The method (this is the actual skill)

**The 25-minute rule:**

| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 0–25 min | Try genuinely. Brute force first — a working slow solution beats no solution |
| 25–35 min | Hints only, not the full solution. Try again |
| 35+ min | Read the editorial. Understand *why*, not just *what* |
| Then | **Close everything. Rewrite from memory.** This is where the learning happens |
| +7 days | Redo it cold. If you can't, you never learned it |

**Every Sunday, re-solve 5 old problems from memory.** This one habit is why some people retain 400 problems and others forget everything from three months ago.

---

## Daily routine

| Phase | Plan |
|---|---|
| Beginner (0–100) | 1 new problem (45 min) + revise 1 old |
| Building (100–300) | 2 new problems (75 min) + revise 2 old |
| Interview prep (300+) | 3 problems **timed** at 45 min each + 1 contest a week |
| Exam week | 1 easy problem. **Never zero.** |

**Never skip two days in a row.** One skipped day is life. Two is the start of quitting.

<details>
<summary><b>Which language is best for DSA?</b></summary>

<br>

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is DSA enough to get placed?</b></summary>

<br>

No. **DSA gets you through the interview; projects get you the interview.** You need both, plus CS fundamentals (OS, DBMS, networks) and — for service companies — aptitude. → [What else you need](../START-HERE.md)
