## The roadmap in one view

```
1. ONE LANGUAGE          C++, Java or Python — pick one, stay 12 months
        ↓
2. DSA (daily, forever)  The only universal filter. Start now, not in final year
        ↓
3. ONE TRACK             Full stack, backend, frontend, QA, mobile, data — pick ONE
        ↓
4. TWO REAL PROJECTS     Deployed, with auth + database. A live URL, not a folder
        ↓
5. CS FUNDAMENTALS       OS · DBMS + SQL · Computer Networks · OOP
        ↓
6. GET VISIBLE           Resume · GitHub · LinkedIn · portfolio
        ↓
7. APPLY AT VOLUME       25+ per week, referrals first. Off-campus is 90% of it
```

## How long does it take?

<div align="center">

| Preparation | Realistic outcome | CTC |
|---|---|:---:|
| 6 months, consistent | Service companies | ₹3.5–7 LPA |
| 12–18 months | Startups and mid product companies | ₹6–15 LPA |
| ~24 months | Strong product companies | ₹15–30 LPA |
| 24–36 months, or switch after 2 yrs | FAANG-level | ₹25–60 LPA |

</div>

"Consistent" means **2+ focused hours daily**, including exam weeks (reduced, never zero). That's ~730 hours a year.

## Step 1 — Pick one language

| Language | Best for | Job market in India |
|---|---|---|
| **Java** | Enterprise, service companies, Android | 🟢 Largest |
| **JavaScript** | Web, startups, full stack | 🟢 Large |
| **Python** | Backend, data, ML, scripting | 🟢 Growing fast |
| **C++** | DSA strength, systems, gaming | 🟡 Smaller but well-paid |

**All four get you hired.** The wrong answer is switching every two months.

**No idea which?** Pick **Full Stack** — most openings, and you discover whether you prefer frontend or backend by doing both.

> [!WARNING]
> **DevOps and ML are traps for freshers.** Both are excellent careers, but very few companies hire freshers directly into them. Enter as a backend developer or data analyst and move across in 1–2 years.

## Step 4 — Projects that count

| ✅ Counts | ❌ Doesn't |
|---|---|
| Live deployed URL | Runs only on your laptop |
| Auth, database, real CRUD | A single static page |
| README with screenshots + setup | No README |
| 30+ commits over weeks | One commit: "final project" |
| You can defend every decision | You followed a tutorial exactly |

**Two or three good projects beat ten weekend clones.** → [Project ideas by track](../projects/README.md)

## Step 5 — Get hired

The part most students underestimate. Your campus brings 3–5 companies; India has thousands.

- **Referrals** — roughly 10× the callback rate of portal applications
- **300 applications** → ~40 responses → ~12 interviews → 2–3 offers. That ratio is normal
- **Start in 3rd year**, not the final semester

## Common questions

<details>
<summary><b>What is the roadmap to become a software engineer in India?</b></summary>

<br>

Learn one programming language well, build DSA alongside it, pick a single track such as full stack or backend, ship two deployed projects, learn CS fundamentals (OS, DBMS, networks, OOP), then apply widely off-campus with a strong resume and GitHub.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How long does it take to become job-ready?</b></summary>

<br>

About 6 months of consistent daily work for service companies, 12–18 months for product companies and startups, and roughly 24 months for strong product companies.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Do I need a degree from a top college?</b></summary>

<br>

No. Online assessments and coding interviews don't show your college name. College brand helps you get campus interviews; skill gets you off-campus interviews — and off-campus is where most of the jobs are.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Should I learn DSA or development first?</b></summary>

<br>

Both, in parallel. DSA gets you through interviews; projects get you the interview. Roughly 45 minutes of DSA and an hour of building each day works well. → [Year-by-year plan](../roadmap/)

</details>
