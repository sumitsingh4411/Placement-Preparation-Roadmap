## The short answer

**Yes, you can get a good software job from a tier-3 college.** Thousands of students do it every year.

But not the way your campus tells you. Here's what actually happens:

- Your placement cell brings 3–5 companies, mostly service companies at ₹3.5–7 LPA
- **90% of your real opportunities are off-campus** — companies that never visit any campus
- Online assessments don't show your college name. They show whether your code passes the test cases

That last point is the whole opportunity. **An OA is a text editor, some test cases and a timer.** It doesn't know your college's NIRF rank, your CGPA, or your English accent.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The realistic path is not "tier-3 → FAANG as a fresher." It's **tier-3 → decent first job → switch after 18–24 months → top company.** Most tier-3 engineers at Google, Amazon and Microsoft got there as experienced hires, not freshers. That route is far more reliable and far less stressful.

## What actually decides your outcome

| It's not | It is |
|---|---|
| Your college's ranking | How many hours you put in, consistently |
| Your CGPA (above 7.0) | Whether you can solve problems and explain your thinking |
| Who visits your campus | How many places you apply to off-campus |
| Luck | Referrals — roughly 10× the callback rate of a portal application |

## The five things tier-3 students get wrong

1. **Waiting for campus placements.** They'll bring you three companies. Off-campus is 90% of your chances. → [Off-campus playbook](../placements/off-campus-strategy.md)
2. **Starting DSA in the final year.** The single most common regret. Two problems a day from second year is 700 problems by graduation.
3. **Applying to 20 places and concluding it doesn't work.** 300 applications is the *baseline* for a placement season, not the extreme.
4. **Rejecting a ₹6 LPA offer while waiting for a dream one.** Take it. Switch in 18 months. Nobody unemployed in June regrets accepting in September.
5. **Only using job portals.** Referrals convert roughly 10× better. Spend most of your effort on people, not forms.

## Common questions

<details>
<summary><b>Can you get a good job from a tier-3 college?</b></summary>

<br>

Yes. Online assessments and coding interviews do not show your college name, so skill is the filter. Most tier-3 students who reach product companies do it off-campus, and many reach top companies by switching jobs after 18–24 months of experience.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How much DSA is needed for placement from a tier-3 college?</b></summary>

<br>

Around 150–200 problems for service companies, 300–400 for mid product companies and startups, and 450–550 for strong product companies. Quality matters more than count — if you can't re-solve a problem two weeks later without help, it doesn't count. → [DSA roadmap](../core/dsa.md)

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>When should a tier-3 student start placement preparation?</b></summary>

<br>

Second year is ideal, because internship applications open during the third year. Starting in third year still works well. Starting in the final semester limits you mostly to service companies — which is still a completely valid route, as long as you plan to switch later.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does CGPA matter for placements?</b></summary>

<br>

Only as a filter. Many companies hard-filter at 6.5 or 7.0, and most service companies require zero active backlogs. Above 7.0, nobody asks about it again after your first job. Below the cutoff, you never get the interview — so keep it above 7.0 and stop worrying about it.

</details>
