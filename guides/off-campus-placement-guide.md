## Why this matters more than campus placements

Your college will bring 3–5 companies. Two will be service companies at ₹3.5 LPA, one will be a product company nobody has heard of, and one will cancel.

**Meanwhile thousands of companies hire freshers off-campus every year and never visit any campus.**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Off-campus is not a backup plan. **It IS the plan.** Campus placement is the bonus. Students who understand this in their 3rd year get multiple offers; students who realise it in April of final year do not.

## The numbers (so you don't panic)

```
300 applications  →  40 responses  →  15 OAs cleared  →  12 interviews  →  2-3 OFFERS
```

**That is a normal, successful placement season — not a failure.**

- Sent **30** applications and got nothing? Your problem is **volume**.

## 1. Referrals — do this the most

A referred application goes into a **separate, much shorter queue**.

**How to get one from a stranger:**

1. **Find alumni from your college** on LinkedIn — highest response rate by far. Also target engineers with 1–4 years experience (more responsive than senior people, and they often get referral bonuses).
2. **Connect with a note.** Never blank.
3. **Wait 1–2 days, then ask** — specifically, with proof of your work.

> Hi [Name], I'm a final-year student at [College] — saw you graduated from there too. I recently built [project] — [one line on what it does]: [live link].
>
> I saw the [role] opening at [Company]. Would you be open to referring me? Happy to share my resume. Thanks either way!

**Expect 1–2 replies per 10 messages. That's a good rate.** Send 15 a week.

## 2. Where to actually apply

<div align="center">

| Platform | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Company career pages** ⭐ | Apply directly, within 48 hrs of a posting |
| **[LinkedIn Jobs](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/)** ⭐ | Set alerts, turn on "Open to Work" |
| **[Instahyre](https://www.instahyre.com/)** | Curated product-company roles |
| **[Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/)** ⭐ | Startups — you often message founders directly |
| **[Unstop](https://unstop.com/)** ⭐ | Hiring challenges — **far less competition** |
| **[Naukri Campus](https://www.naukri.com/campus)** | Fresher mass-hiring drives |
| **Twitter/X** ⭐ | Many startups post only here. Underused by students |

</div>

> [!TIP]
> **Apply within the first 48 hours of a posting.** Recruiters often stop reviewing after ~200 applications.

## 3. Cold email — genuinely works at startups

Founders read their own inbox. A CTO at a 20-person company will open a well-written email from a student.

**Subject:** Final-year CS student — built [thing relevant to their product]

> Hi [Name], I've been using [their product] and noticed [specific, genuine observation].
>
> I'm a final-year CS student. I recently built [project]: [live link] · [GitHub].
>
> I'd love to join as a [role]. I know you may not have a posted opening — happy to do a paid or unpaid trial task so you can see how I work.
>
> Resume: [link]. Thanks for reading.

**Send 5 a week.**

## Common questions

<details>
<summary><b>How do freshers get off-campus jobs?</b></summary>

<br>

Mostly through referrals, direct applications on company career pages, curated portals such as Instahyre and Wellfound, cold emails to startups, and hiring challenges on Unstop and HackerEarth. Referrals convert best by a wide margin.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>How many applications does it take to get a job?</b></summary>

<br>

Roughly 300 applications produce about 40 responses, 12 interviews and 2–3 offers. That ratio is normal, not a sign of failure. If 30 applications got you nothing, the issue is volume.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Can I get a job after graduating without placement?</b></summary>

<br>

Yes. Companies hire year-round, and many people get their first job 3–8 months after graduating. Keep building something so your resume has no unexplained gap, and be direct about what you did with the time.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is off-campus placement harder than on-campus?</b></summary>

<br>

The bar per company is similar, but you compete with more people and have to generate your own opportunities. In exchange you get access to thousands of companies instead of five — which is why most tier-3 offers come from off-campus.

</details>
