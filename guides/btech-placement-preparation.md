## The four things you prepare in parallel

Most students do these one after another and run out of time. Do them **together**, in different daily blocks.

<div align="center">

| # | What | Daily time | Why |
|:---:|---|:---:|---|
| 1️⃣ | **[DSA](../core/dsa.md)** | 45–90 min | The only universal filter. Every product company tests it |
| 2️⃣ | **One track + projects** | 60 min | Gets you the interview in the first place |
| 3️⃣ | **[CS fundamentals](../core/cs-fundamentals.md)** | 30 min | OS, DBMS, CN, OOP — asked in every technical round |
| 4️⃣ | **[Aptitude](../core/aptitude-and-maths.md)** | 45 min *(final year)* | Round 1 at every service company |

</div>

> [!TIP]
> **Your syllabus already contains #3.** OS, DBMS, CN and OOP are in your B.Tech course. Study them the way interviews ask ("when would you use X?") instead of the way exams ask, and you pass both from one effort.

## When to start

| Year | Priority | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | Learn one language properly. Basic DSA. Git + Linux. Target 150 problems. | [Year 1 →](../roadmap/year-1.md) |
| **2nd** ⭐ | **The make-or-break year.** Pick one track, ship a deployed project, reach 300 problems. | [Year 2 →](../roadmap/year-2.md) |
| **3rd** | Internship, 2 portfolio projects, resume + LinkedIn, 500 problems. | [Year 3 →](../roadmap/year-3.md) |
| **4th** | Stop learning new things. Apply 25+/week. Revise and interview. | [Year 4 →](../roadmap/year-4.md) |

**Behind?** There's a compressed plan for 4, 8 and 18 months → **[late-start.md](../roadmap/late-start.md)**

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Internship applications open in your 3rd year, around July–September** — nearly a year before the internship itself. Most students discover this in March and find everything closed.

## Which subjects matter for placement?

<div align="center">

| Subject | Interview weight | What they actually ask |
|---|:---:|---|
| **Data Structures & Algorithms** | 🔴 Highest | Live coding, 45 min per problem |
| **DBMS + SQL** | 🔴 High | Normalisation, ACID, indexing, live queries |
| **Operating Systems** | 🟠 High | Process vs thread, deadlock, virtual memory |
| **OOP** | 🟠 High | 4 pillars, abstract class vs interface, SOLID |
| **Computer Networks** | 🟠 Medium-High | TCP vs UDP, HTTP, DNS, "what happens when you type a URL" |
| **System Design** | 🟡 Medium | Only for strong product companies / 2+ YOE |

</div>

## The eligibility rules nobody tells you

| Requirement | Reality |
|---|---|
| **CGPA above 7.0** | Many companies hard-filter at 6.5 or 7.0. Cheapest requirement to satisfy |
| **Zero active backlogs** | Most service companies and many product companies require this **at joining**. A single carried backlog can void an offer |
| **Consistent branch/degree** | Some companies restrict by branch — check before you invest time |

> [!WARNING]
> **Backlogs are the silent career killer.** Clear them immediately. Do not let one carry into your final year.

## On-campus vs off-campus

Your campus will bring 3–5 companies. **India has thousands hiring freshers off-campus every year.**

- **300 applications** → ~40 responses → ~12 interviews → 2–3 offers
- **Referrals convert ~10× better** than portal applications
- Start applying in **3rd year**, not the final semester

## Common questions

<details>
<summary><b>How to prepare for placement in B.Tech?</b></summary>

<br>

Work on four things in parallel: DSA daily, one career track with real deployed projects, CS fundamentals, and aptitude if you're targeting service companies. Then apply off-campus at volume from third year onwards.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Which subjects are important for placement?</b></summary>

<br>

Data structures and algorithms, operating systems, DBMS with SQL, computer networks and object-oriented programming. All of these are already in your B.Tech syllabus.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>When should B.Tech students start placement preparation?</b></summary>

<br>

Second year is ideal, because internship applications open in the third year. First year is even better if you can manage it. Third year still works. Final semester limits you mostly to service companies.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is CGPA important for placements?</b></summary>

<br>

As a filter, yes — many companies cut off at 6.5 or 7.0, and most require zero active backlogs at joining. Above that threshold it stops mattering, and nobody asks after your first job.

</details>
