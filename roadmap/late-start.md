## First: the honest version

**You have lost time. That is real, and pretending otherwise doesn't help you.**

You cannot compress 3 years into 3 months. Anyone telling you "crack FAANG in 90 days" is selling something.

**But here's what's also true:** the bar for a *first* job is much lower than the internet suggests. To get a solid ₹4–8 LPA job you need maybe **250 DSA problems, one good project, decent fundamentals, and the ability to talk.** That is **6–8 months of honest daily work.** Not 3 years.

Thousands of people start in their 3rd year and get placed. What kills students isn't a late start — it's spending the remaining time panicking, comparing themselves to others, and jumping between roadmaps instead of following one.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The plan below assumes 4–6 hours a day.** That's the cost of starting late. If you can only manage 2, stretch the timeline by 50% and target service companies first — a completely respectable path.

## Plan A — 3rd year (18 months)

**You are in a good position. You have enough time to reach product companies.** Do not waste it feeling behind.

### Months 1–2: Foundation sprint

- [ ] Pick **one** language: C++, Java, or Python. Never change it.
- [ ] Syntax → loops → functions → arrays → strings → OOP. 3 weeks max, then move on.
- [ ] Git + GitHub basics → [core/git-and-linux.md](../core/git-and-linux.md)
- [ ] Solve **50 easy problems**. They'll be brutal. Push through.

### Months 3–6: DSA hard push + track

- [ ] **4 problems a day.** Non-negotiable. → [core/dsa.md](../core/dsa.md)
- [ ] Cover: arrays, strings, hashing, two pointers, sliding window, binary search, recursion, linked lists, stacks/queues, trees, basic graphs
- [ ] Pick your track → [tracks/README.md](../tracks/README.md) *(pick **Full Stack** or **QA/SDET** if unsure — fastest to employable)*
- [ ] Learn your track's core stack — one course, then **rebuild the project without it**
- [ ] **Target: 250 problems by month 6**

### Months 7–10: Projects + fundamentals

- [ ] Build and deploy **project #1** (6 weeks, real features, auth + database)
- [ ] Build and deploy **project #2** (4 weeks, different from #1)
- [ ] CS fundamentals: OS, DBMS, CN, OOP → [core/cs-fundamentals.md](../core/cs-fundamentals.md)
- [ ] SQL — 50 queries by hand
- [ ] DSA continues: graphs + DP. **Target: 400 problems**

### Months 11–14: Get visible

- [ ] Resume, LinkedIn, GitHub → [placements/resume.md](../placements/resume.md)
- [ ] Apply for internships — **15/week** → [placements/internships.md](../placements/internships.md)
- [ ] Weekly mock interviews
- [ ] Aptitude prep if targeting service companies
- [ ] **Target: 500 problems**

### Months 15–18: Convert

- [ ] Apply to full-time roles — **25/week** → [placements/off-campus-strategy.md](../placements/off-campus-strategy.md)
- [ ] System design basics → [core/system-design.md](../core/system-design.md)
- [ ] Revise everything, interview constantly

**Realistic outcome:** ₹6–15 LPA at a product company or good startup. FAANG is possible but don't build your plan around it.

## Plan B — 4th year (8 months)

**Time is tight. Ruthless prioritisation only.** Cut anything that isn't directly hire-relevant.

### What you DO

<div align="center">

| Priority | Task | Daily time |
|:---:|---|:---:|
| 🔴 **1** | DSA — arrays, strings, hashing, two pointers, binary search, recursion, linked lists, stacks, trees, basic graphs, basic DP | 2.5 hrs |
| 🔴 **2** | ONE good deployed project (auth + database + real features) | 1.5 hrs |
| 🟠 **3** | CS fundamentals — OS, DBMS, CN, OOP (interview-level, not exam-level) | 45 min |
| 🟠 **4** | Aptitude — if targeting service companies (they're your safety net) | 45 min |
| 🟡 **5** | Applications + referrals — from month 2 onwards | 30 min |

### What you SKIP (be honest about it)

❌ Competitive programming · ❌ Segment trees, advanced DP, tries · ❌ Deep system design · ❌ Learning 3 frameworks · ❌ Open source · ❌ 5 projects · ❌ Blogging/personal branding

### The 8-month schedule

| Month | Focus | Cumulative problems |
|:---:|---|:---:|
| **1** | Language + arrays, strings, hashing. Start applying to service companies. | 60 |
| **2** | Two pointers, sliding window, binary search, recursion. Aptitude starts. | 130 |
| **3** | Linked lists, stacks, queues. **Start project #1.** | 190 |
| **4** | Trees + BST. Project continues. Resume + LinkedIn done. | 250 |
| **5** | Basic graphs + basic DP. **Project deployed.** Apply 25/week. | 300 |
| **6** | CS fundamentals sprint. Mock interviews weekly. Interviewing actively. | 340 |

**Realistic outcome:** service companies (₹3.5–7 LPA) are very achievable. Startups and mid product companies (₹6–12 LPA) are achievable if you execute well. Take the first decent offer, then switch in 18 months.

## Plan C — 4th year, final semester

**~4 months. Target: get employed. Optimise for probability, not prestige.**

### Priority order

1. **Aptitude + basic coding** (2 hrs/day) — this is what service companies test, and they hire in thousands. → [core/aptitude-and-maths.md](../core/aptitude-and-maths.md)
2. **150 easy/medium DSA problems** (2 hrs/day) — arrays, strings, hashing, sorting, searching, basic recursion
3. **One deployed project** (1 hr/day) — even a solid CRUD app with auth. Something to talk about.
4. **Apply everywhere, immediately** (1 hr/day) — 30+ per week from week 1
5. **Interview communication** — practise explaining your project and your thinking out loud

### Where to focus your applications

<div align="center">

| Target | Why | Difficulty |
|---|---|:---:|
| **Service companies** — TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant, Accenture, Capgemini, LTIMindtree | Mass hiring, aptitude-focused, thousands of seats | 🟢 |
| **QA / Testing roles** | Much lower competition, real career path | 🟢 |
| **Technical support / Cloud support** | Big teams, always hiring | 🟢 |
| **Small startups (10–50 people)** | Care about attitude and shipping, not pedigree | 🟡 |
| **BPO/KPO tech roles** | Foot in the door; switch to dev in 1–2 years | 🟢 |

</div>

> [!TIP]
> **TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro and Cognizant are your best friends right now.** They hire in bulk, weight aptitude heavily, and don't care about your college tier. Register for every single one. Getting in and switching in 2 years is a completely normal, successful career — a very large share of Indian senior engineers started exactly there.

## Plan D — Already graduated

**You have the best asset of all: 8–10 hours a day with no classes.** A committed 6-month sprint here beats 3 distracted college years.

### The rules

- [ ] Treat job prep as a **full-time job**: 9 AM–6 PM, fixed schedule, weekdays
- [ ] **Fill the gap on your resume.** Never leave it blank. Build in public, freelance, contribute to open source, do certifications — anything you can point to
- [ ] Follow **Plan B compressed into 4–5 months** (you have double the daily hours)
- [ ] Apply to roles that don't filter on graduation year — **startups care far less than big companies do**
- [ ] Freelance in parallel — Upwork, Fiverr, local businesses. Income *and* resume lines
- [ ] Consider paid-but-legitimate routes: Masai, AltCampus, Scaler (research honestly, avoid ISA traps and "guaranteed placement" scams)

### Explaining the gap in interviews

> "After graduating I realised my fundamentals weren't strong enough for the roles I wanted, so I spent [X] months building them properly — [N] DSA problems, [project], and [freelance/OSS work]. Here's what I built: [link]."

**Own it directly.** Interviewers respect a candidate who identified a weakness and fixed it far more than one who makes excuses. What they don't respect is a vague, unexplained blank.

## 🚫 What NOT to do when you're behind

| Don't | Instead |
|---|---|
| **Try to do everything at once** | Pick the 3 highest-impact things. Ignore the rest. |
| **Spend ₹1–3 lakh on a "placement guarantee" course** | Everything here is free. Most of those courses teach exactly this and take your first year's salary. |
| **Wait to feel "ready" before applying** | Apply now. Interviews are the fastest way to find your gaps. |
| **Compare yourself to tier-1 students** | Different starting line. Compare only to yourself last month. |
| **Give up because "tier-3 mein kuch nahi hota"** | Thousands of tier-3 students got hired this year. Selection bias is not destiny. |
| **Do 14 hours on Sunday and nothing all week** | 4 consistent hours beats 14 sporadic ones, always. |
| **Learn a new language because it's "trending"** | Whatever you know now is fine. Depth beats novelty. |
| **Hide your college in interviews** | Nobody cares once you can code. Confidence about it matters more than the name. |

## 🧠 If you're panicking right now

Read this slowly:

- **Your first job is not your last job.** The average engineer changes jobs every 2 years. Your starting point matters far less than your trajectory.
- **A ₹4 LPA job is not a failure.** It's a starting salary that typically becomes ₹12–15 LPA within 4 years if you keep learning. Almost nobody starts at the top.
- **Getting placed 6 months after graduation is normal.** Nobody will ask about it after your first job.
- **The panic is worse than the work.** Sitting down and solving one problem right now will feel better than another hour of reading roadmaps.

**Do this in the next 10 minutes:**

1. Open [LeetCode](https://leetcode.com/problemset/) → filter Easy → pick any problem
2. Try for 25 minutes
3. If stuck, read the solution, then rewrite it from memory
4. That's day 1. Tomorrow, do day 2.

**That's it. There is no better plan than starting.**
