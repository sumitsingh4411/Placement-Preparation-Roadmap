## Why this matters so much for you

**A tier-3 student with a 6-month internship beats a tier-1 student with none, in most fresher hiring loops.**

An internship changes what your resume *is*. It moves you from "student who did projects" to "person who has worked on real software with real users, in a team, under review." That's a different category of candidate.

**What it gets you:**
- ✅ **A PPO** (pre-placement offer) — the easiest good offer you will ever receive
- ✅ Real experience on your resume, which every subsequent recruiter weighs heavily
- ✅ A professional network and a LinkedIn recommendation
- ✅ Proof you can work in a team, use Git properly, and ship under a deadline
- ✅ Clarity about what you actually enjoy doing

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A PPO is the cheapest path to a good job.** No OA lottery, no 5 rounds, no 300 applications. Treat every internship as a 3-month interview — because that is exactly what it is.

## When to apply

<div align="center">

| Your year | What to target | Apply when |
|---|---|---|
| **After year 1** | Anything — unpaid, small startup, open source | Mar–May |
| **After year 2** ⭐ | Real paid internship, small-to-mid company | Jan–Apr |
| **Year 3 (during)** | Part-time / remote internship alongside classes | Jul–Sep |
| **After year 3** ⭐⭐ | **The big one** — product company summer internship, PPO potential | **Aug–Dec** (year before) |
| **Year 4** | 6-month full-time internship (many convert to FTE) | Jul–Oct |

</div>

> [!WARNING]
> **Summer internship applications for the year-3 summer open in July–September of your 3rd year — nearly a full year in advance.** Most students discover this in March and find everything closed. Mark your calendar now.

## 📩 Cold outreach

<details>
<summary><b>Templates that get replies</b></summary>

<br>

**LinkedIn DM — asking for a referral**

> Hi [Name], I'm a 3rd-year CS student and I've been following [Company]'s work on [specific thing].
>
> I recently built [project] — [one line on what it does] using [stack]: [live link].
>
> I saw the [role] internship opening. Would you be open to referring me, or pointing me to the right person? Happy to share my resume.
>
> Thanks for your time either way.

**Cold email to a startup founder**

**Subject:** 3rd-year CS student — built [something relevant to their product]

> Hi [Name],
>
> I've been using [their product] and noticed [genuine specific observation].
>
> I'm a 3rd-year CS student looking for a [role] internship. I recently built [project]: [live link] · [GitHub link] — [one line on the interesting technical part].
>
> I know you may not have a posted internship role, but I'd love to contribute. Happy to do a paid or unpaid trial task so you can see how I work.
>
> Resume: [link]
>
> Thanks for reading,
> [Name]

**Why these work:** specific to them, proof before ask, short, easy to say no to. **Send 10 a week. Expect 1–2 replies.** That's a good rate.

</details>

## 💰 Should you take an unpaid internship?

<div align="center">

| Situation | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Real work, real mentorship, no stipend, you're in year 1–2** | ✅ **Take it.** The resume line and experience are worth more than the stipend |
| **You have a paid offer too** | ✅ Take the paid one |
| **They want you to pay THEM for a "certificate"** | ❌ **Scam. Walk away.** |
| **"Unpaid trial" that lasts 3+ months with no offer** | ❌ You're being used as free labour |
| **Sales/marketing work labelled a "tech internship"** | ❌ No technical learning, no resume value |
| **You're in your final year and need income** | 🟡 Prefer paid, but a good unpaid role beats no experience |

</div>

> [!WARNING]
> **Never pay for an internship.** Legitimate companies do not charge you to work. Any "internship" requiring a registration fee, training fee, or certificate fee is a scam. This includes many programmes advertised aggressively on Instagram.
>
> **Also avoid:** "internships" that are just watching recorded videos and getting a certificate. They carry zero weight and recruiters recognise them instantly.

## ✅ How to actually get selected

**1. Have something to show.** One deployed project with a live link beats a perfectly formatted resume with nothing behind it.

**2. Apply early and in volume.** 15–20 applications a week during the season. Most students send 10 total.

**3. Prioritise referrals.** 10× the conversion of a portal application.

**4. Tailor your application.** Mention the company by name and something specific about them. Mass-blasted generic applications get filtered.

**5. Be willing to start small.** A 15-person startup gives you more real responsibility than a big company's intern programme — and it's far easier to get.

**6. Offer a trial task.** "Happy to do a small paid task so you can see how I work" converts surprisingly often at startups.

**7. Contribute to open source.** Maintainers notice, and it's public proof of your ability to work in someone else's codebase.

## 🚀 Making the internship count

**Getting it is half. Converting it is the other half.**

### Week 1
- [ ] Set up your environment, get access to everything
- [ ] Read the codebase — understand the architecture before changing anything
- [ ] Learn the team's Git workflow and code review process
- [ ] Ask your manager: **"What does a successful internship look like to you?"** ⭐ *(Ask this on day one. It tells you exactly what you're being measured on.)*

### Throughout
- [ ] **Ship real features.** Not just bug fixes — ask for something meaningful
- [ ] **Ask questions**, but try for 30 minutes first, then ask with what you've already tried
- [ ] **Write things down** — every task, every decision, every metric. You need this for your resume
- [ ] **Be reliable.** Meeting deadlines matters more than being brilliant
- [ ] Take code review feedback well — it's the fastest learning available anywhere
- [ ] Talk to people outside your immediate team
- [ ] Volunteer for the unglamorous work nobody wants. It's how interns get remembered

### Before you leave ⭐
- [ ] **Ask directly about a PPO.** Do not assume it will be offered — many PPOs go to whoever asked
- [ ] **Get a LinkedIn recommendation from your manager** *(ask while you're still there — response rates drop to near-zero after you leave)*
- [ ] Collect metrics for your resume: features shipped, bugs fixed, performance improvements, test coverage
- [ ] Connect with everyone you worked with on LinkedIn
- [ ] Send a thank-you note to your manager and mentor
- [ ] Ask for feedback: "What should I work on to be a stronger engineer?"

<details>
<summary><b>💬 How to ask for a PPO</b></summary>

<br>

**Timing:** 3–4 weeks before your internship ends. Not on the last day.

> "I've really enjoyed working on [specific thing] this summer, and I'd love to continue with the team full time. Is there a possibility of a pre-placement offer? And if so, is there anything specific I should focus on in my remaining weeks?"

**Why this works:** it's direct, it shows genuine interest in the work rather than just the job, and the second question invites them to tell you exactly what to do to earn it.

**If they say no or "we don't know yet":** ask for a referral instead — *"Completely understood. Would you be comfortable referring me when full-time roles open, or recommending me to another team?"* A manager who liked your work will usually say yes.

</details>

## ⚠️ Internship mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| **Applying in March for a summer internship** | Apply Aug–Dec of the previous year. The good ones close early. |
| **Only applying to big-name companies** | Startups hire far more interns and give you more real work. |
| **Refusing unpaid roles in year 1–2** | Your first line of experience is worth more than ₹5,000/month. |
| **Paying for an "internship"** | Always a scam. Walk away. |
| **Treating it as a formality** | It's a 3-month interview. Behave accordingly. |
| **Not asking for a PPO** | Ask explicitly. Many PPOs go to whoever raised it. |
| **Not getting a recommendation before leaving** | Ask while you're still there. |
| **Not recording your metrics** | You'll forget. Write down numbers weekly. |
| **Being invisible** | Speak up in standups, ask questions, volunteer. Quiet interns get forgotten at PPO time. |
